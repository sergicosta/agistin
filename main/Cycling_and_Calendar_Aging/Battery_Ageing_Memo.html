01

Objective

Replace the previous time-only degradation model with a combined calendar + cycling ageing model, so battery health predictions depend on current (charge/discharge), State of Charge, and temperature — not elapsed time alone.

The two mechanisms are modelled separately and then summed, matching the source paper's own construction: a battery loses capacity both from sitting (calendar ageing) and from being used (cycling ageing).

02

Formulas implemented

Calendar ageing

Ln(Q_cal) = f · e^(g·SoC) · e^(h/T) · t_days^z
Depends on SoC, constant T, elapsed time

Cycling ageing

Ln(Q_cyc) = |a·T² + b·T + c| · e^((d·T+e)·Crate) · AH
Depends on current (via C-rate, Ah), constant T

Combined into a single State of Health: SOH = 1 − (Q_cal + Q_cyc) / 100

Over 24 months, cycling ageing accounts for the large majority of total capacity loss. This is consistent with the battery being held at low State of Charge for most of the horizon in this scenario — which the model correctly predicts as suppressing calendar ageing.

05

Known limitation

Numerical sensitivity in the cycling term The coefficient (a·T² + b·T + c) is a near-cancellation of three very small numbers. Using only the ~5 significant figures published in the paper's table causes this term to flip sign at typical operating temperatures — an artifact not present in the paper's own figures. A magnitude-preserving correction (abs()) was applied and documented in code. Higher-precision parameters from the underlying PhD thesis (ref. [21] in the paper) would remove the need for this safeguard.
